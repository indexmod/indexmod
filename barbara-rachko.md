**Barbara Rachko** is an American contemporary artist based in New York City, known for large-scale pastel-on-sandpaper paintings that transform folk-art figures, masks, toys, and ritual objects into staged, psychologically charged compositions. Her work is associated with long-running series including **Domestic Threats**, **Black Paintings**, and **Bolivianos**. **[1]**

## Background

Rachko was born in Paterson, New Jersey, in 1953, according to Vedica Art Studios and Gallery, which represents her work. She is based in New York and has described a career path that included aviation and military service before she became a full-time visual artist. **[2]** In a 2021 interview, she discussed earning pilot credentials, serving in the Navy, and later choosing to leave that career to pursue art full time. **[3]**

Her official biography and artist materials identify her as a pastel artist whose work has been developed over more than three decades. The practice is centered on soft pastel applied to sandpaper, a surface that allows dense color, layered marks, and highly saturated images. **[1]**

## Work

Rachko's paintings often use Mexican and Guatemalan folk-art objects, including masks, carved wooden animals, papier-mache figures, and toys. Rather than presenting those objects as documentary records, she stages them as actors in constructed scenes. Her imagery combines observation, memory, theatricality, and symbolic narrative. **[1]**

The **Domestic Threats** series began in 1992 and continued until 2007. It used figures from Mexican folk art in settings that blended reality and fantasy. After that series, Rachko developed photographic work and the **Black Paintings**, in which cultural objects and figures appear against dark grounds. **[1]**

In 2017 she began the **Bolivianos** series after seeing Bolivian Carnival masks at the National Museum of Ethnography and Folklore in La Paz. The series extended her interest in masks and ritual forms into a new body of pastel paintings. **[1]**

## Exhibitions And Recognition

Rachko has presented solo exhibitions in New York and elsewhere, including *Barbara Rachko: Gods and Monsters* at HP Garcia Gallery in New York in 2009 and *Barbara Rachko: The Black Paintings* at Gallery Gray in Minneapolis in 2011. Her official CV also lists earlier solo exhibitions at La MaMa La Galleria, Roanoke College, the Torpedo Factory Art Center, and other venues. **[4]**

Her work has appeared in group exhibitions and art fairs in the United States, Europe, and Asia. Vedica Art Studios and Gallery lists later presentations connected to Moon Art Fair Hamburg, NOISE Media Art Fair in Vienna, and exhibitions in Mumbai and Paris. **[2]**

The short documentary *Barbara Rachko: True Grit*, directed by Jennifer Cox, premiered at the Newport Beach Film Festival in 2023, where the artist's official site states that it won the Audience Award and Best in Category for Art, Architecture, and Design. **[1]**

## Themes

Rachko's work is shaped by the transformation of collected objects into pictorial characters. Her paintings often create a space between still life, portraiture, theatrical staging, and psychological narrative. The use of masks is central: masks can appear as cultural objects, symbolic bodies, or unstable identities.

Her pastel technique is also a defining part of the work. By using sandpaper as a support, she builds dense color and strong contrast, especially in the darker compositions of the **Black Paintings**. The result is a practice that treats pastel not as a preparatory or secondary medium, but as a finished form of contemporary painting.
