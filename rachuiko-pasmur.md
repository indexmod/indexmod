**Pasmur Rachuiko** (Russian: Пасмур Рачуйко; born 1986, Rostov-on-Don) is a Russian contemporary artist whose work is associated with **naive surrealism**, invented mythologies, folk imagery, and psychedelic fantasy. His practice includes painting, graphic work, animation, music, and projects connected with edible wild plants in urban environments. **[1]**

{{page:image}}

*Image: Radio Svoboda. Source: https://www.svoboda.org/a/30733477.html*

## Biography

Rachuiko was born in Rostov-on-Don in 1986. RAAN records that he studied at the St. Petersburg State University of Culture and Arts and later became active in exhibitions, artist residencies, and interdisciplinary projects. **[2]** In an interview with *Kto Glavny*, he described growing up in Rostov-on-Don, moving between cities, and developing as a self-taught painter outside a conventional academic painting path. **[3]**

His public biography often appears under the name **Pasmur**, a pseudonym connected with his artistic identity. His work combines figures, animals, plants, ritual motifs, and imaginary characters, creating a personal visual mythology rather than documentary realism. **[1]**

## Work

Vladey describes Rachuiko's painting as naive surrealism and notes that his images are populated by strange beings from a personal epos, mixing psychedelic fantasy with ordinary reality. **[1]** This combination gives the works an intentionally unstable tone: humorous, folkloric, grotesque, and sometimes mystical.

One recurring direction in Rachuiko's practice is his interest in edible plants and fungi found in cities. In the Radio Svoboda interview, he discussed parks, wild food, slugs, and urban gathering as part of a broader artistic and everyday practice. **[4]** The project **Wild Feed** developed this interest into a public format involving urban ecology, food, walking, and artistic research. **[2]**

In addition to painting, Rachuiko has worked with animation, video, and music. RAAN lists *Another Face of Nefertiti*, a series of animations created with Dima Libin in 2021, and notes Rachuiko's participation in exhibitions and projects at venues including the Gogol Center and Street Art Museum. **[2]**

## Exhibitions And Projects

Rachuiko's exhibition history includes group and solo projects in Saint Petersburg, Moscow, Rostov-on-Don, and other cities. The original article listed several early exhibitions, including *DAO DAG: Contemporary Art from Dagestan* at Gallery LUDA in Saint Petersburg in 2015, *Selfportraits* at the Brewhouse Contemporary Art Center in Moscow in 2016, and *Brighter Days Are Coming* at the Street Art Museum in Saint Petersburg in 2017.

Vladey and RAAN also connect his work with later cultural projects, including collaborations and public programmes related to contemporary Russian art, theater, and independent exhibition spaces. **[1]** **[2]**
