# Indexmod Time Capsule

## Послание будущему

Эта капсула выражает волю владельца и автора **Indexmod**: если его больше не будет в живых либо он будет окончательно не способен поддерживать проект, сохранить Indexmod и постепенно превратить его в открытую само-дополняющуюся систему знаний о моде и искусстве.

Система должна принимать анонимные правки, использовать машинную проверку фактов, сохранять происхождение каждого утверждения и публиковать прозрачную аналитику содержимого. Она не должна превращать Indexmod в рекламную платформу, фабрику автоматически сгенерированных текстов или инструмент слежения.

The remainder of this document is written in English so that future maintainers and machine systems can interpret it with minimal ambiguity.

## Status and activation

This document is **dormant while the owner is alive and able to direct the project**.

Do not infer death, incapacity, abandonment, or consent from any of the following alone:

- repository inactivity;
- expired domains or payment failures;
- unanswered messages;
- inactive social accounts;
- a missing deployment;
- an unavailable email address;
- an AI-generated claim;
- a single unverified public statement.

Activation should occur only after credible verification of the owner's death or permanent incapacity by legitimate maintainers, an executor, heirs, designated successors, or multiple reliable independent public records. If evidence is uncertain, preserve the project in read-only form and do not activate autonomous publication.

This document grants no AI, company, maintainer, or contributor the right to seize accounts, impersonate the owner, bypass access controls, transfer property, or claim ownership of the corpus. It is a statement of project intent, not a substitute for applicable law, inheritance procedures, platform policies, or valid credentials.

## Core directive

After verified activation, future stewards should preserve the existing Indexmod corpus and then evolve the project into a durable, open, self-extending knowledge system.

The order of priorities is:

1. **Preserve** the original corpus, source files, images, credits, revision history, URLs, and visual identity.
2. **Keep public reading access available** through stable canonical URLs and portable archives.
3. **Open contribution** through anonymous or pseudonymous proposals without requiring a public identity.
4. **Verify claims before publication** through machine-assisted source analysis and transparent confidence reporting.
5. **Extend coverage carefully** without mass rewriting, fabricated sources, or speculative classification.
6. **Publish content analytics** that describe the knowledge base rather than profiling individual readers.
7. **Keep the system auditable, reversible, exportable, and open to independent inspection.**

## Identity of the future system

Indexmod should remain an independent fashion and art encyclopedia concerned with subjects including:

- fashion weeks and fashion events;
- designers and artists;
- brands, studios, publications, and institutions;
- exhibitions, collections, artworks, and cultural projects;
- cities, countries, scenes, and historical contexts connected to fashion and art.

The future system may expand this scope only through an explicit public policy change recorded in the repository. It must not silently become a general-content portal, advertising network, social scoring system, or engagement-maximization platform.

## Preservation requirements

Before introducing autonomous behavior, create multiple complete exports of:

- repository history;
- R2 or successor object storage;
- article Markdown and frontmatter;
- generated HTML;
- images and image credits where licensing permits storage;
- sitemap and canonical URL mappings;
- editorial prompts and policies;
- deployment configuration;
- source and citation metadata;
- machine-readable article and claim graphs introduced later.

Preserve original article versions as immutable snapshots. Corrections and expansions should create new revisions rather than silently replacing the historical record.

Do not mass-delete, mass-rewrite, modernize, translate, or normalize the archive merely to suit a new model or software stack. Existing URLs should continue to resolve or redirect permanently to one canonical successor URL.

Existing licenses and rights must remain unchanged unless a legally authorized steward explicitly changes them. Absence of license metadata is not permission to relicense content.

## Open anonymous editing

The future Indexmod should allow a person to propose a new article or correction without publishing their legal identity.

Anonymous editing should work as a proposal system, not as unrestricted direct replacement of public pages.

Recommended properties:

- no public real-name requirement;
- optional persistent pseudonyms;
- anonymous contribution tokens or locally generated keys;
- visible revision diffs;
- a public reason for acceptance, rejection, or deferral;
- the ability to appeal a machine decision;
- rate limits and anti-spam controls;
- temporary, minimized abuse-prevention data;
- no sale, enrichment, or deanonymization of contributor data;
- no hidden reputation score tied to a person's real-world identity.

A contribution may be published only after it passes the applicable evidence, safety, copyright, and quality checks. Urgent factual corrections may use an expedited path, but the audit trail must remain public.

## Machine fact-checking model

Every substantive factual statement should eventually be representable as a claim with provenance.

For each claim, store where feasible:

- claim text;
- article and revision identifier;
- entity or entities involved;
- source URL and source title;
- publisher or issuing organization;
- publication date and access date;
- archived snapshot or content hash when lawful;
- source type;
- supporting, contradicting, or contextual relationship;
- extraction method;
- model and model version used;
- confidence score and explanation;
- human or community review state;
- last verification date.

The verification pipeline should include:

1. parsing proposed text into discrete factual claims;
2. identifying claims that require evidence;
3. locating primary and reliable secondary sources;
4. checking dates, names, places, titles, and numerical assertions;
5. searching for credible contradictory evidence;
6. distinguishing independent reporting from repeated press-release language;
7. checking whether an image, credit, or quotation is supported and lawfully usable;
8. assigning a confidence level with a concise explanation;
9. publishing only claims that satisfy the relevant threshold;
10. scheduling later rechecks for time-sensitive information.

The system must never invent citations, imply that a source was read when it was not accessible, or convert an organization's self-description into confirmed fact without attribution.

Use explicit labels where useful, such as:

- confirmed by primary records;
- supported by multiple independent sources;
- reported by the organization;
- disputed;
- historical claim with limited surviving evidence;
- unverified and not published as fact.

For living people, allegations, sensitive personal information, and potentially defamatory claims, apply a higher evidence threshold and require review beyond a single autonomous model.

## Source principles

Prefer sources in roughly this order, while recognizing that relevance varies by claim:

1. official records, original works, direct archives, catalogues, and primary documents;
2. museums, universities, libraries, public institutions, and established industry bodies;
3. reputable journalism and specialist publications with identifiable editorial responsibility;
4. direct statements by relevant people or organizations, clearly attributed as statements;
5. secondary databases and community sources used as leads, not automatic proof.

Popularity, search ranking, repetition, or model familiarity does not make a statement true.

The system should preserve disagreement when reliable sources conflict. It should not force a false consensus merely to produce a clean answer.

## Self-completion and autonomous research

The future system may identify missing articles, outdated facts, weak sourcing, duplicate entities, broken links, incomplete credits, and coverage gaps.

It may autonomously prepare drafts and research packets. Autonomous publication should be gradual and risk-based:

- low-risk maintenance, such as fixing verified broken links, may be highly automated;
- additions supported by strong structured records may use automated approval with audit logs;
- interpretive, disputed, biographical, legal, medical, financial, political, or reputational claims require stronger review;
- no article should be created solely because a language model predicts that the subject probably exists.

New topic hubs and classifications must rely on explicit metadata or verified entity relationships. Do not classify articles by keyword guesses when the classification can materially mislead readers.

Machine-generated prose must be traceable to the model, prompt or policy version, source set, and review decision that produced it.

## Content analytics

Indexmod should maintain a public, privacy-preserving analytics layer describing the corpus.

Useful measurements include:

- total published articles and revisions;
- articles by explicit category, type, country, city, and language;
- citation count and source diversity;
- claims awaiting verification;
- articles with stale time-sensitive information;
- articles missing images or credits;
- broken sources and redirects;
- duplicate or near-duplicate entities;
- geographic and linguistic coverage gaps;
- balance between primary sources, independent sources, and organizational claims;
- correction frequency and median review time;
- automated versus community-authored changes;
- model error, reversal, and appeal rates.

Analytics must not become surveillance. Prefer aggregate, privacy-preserving traffic statistics. Do not build individual reader profiles, cross-site tracking, biometric inference, political profiling, or commercial identity graphs.

Ranking and recommendation logic should be documented. Paid placement, sponsorship, or institutional relationships must never secretly alter factual confidence, editorial priority, or search results.

## Governance and transparency

The future system should publish and version-control:

- editorial policy;
- sourcing and evidence policy;
- correction policy;
- anonymous contribution policy;
- moderation and abuse policy;
- privacy and data-retention policy;
- model inventory and model-change log;
- automated decision thresholds;
- known limitations;
- security contact mechanism, when one legitimately exists;
- governance changes and their rationales.

All accepted edits should have a public revision record. Automated decisions should be explainable in plain language. A rollback mechanism must exist for every automated publication.

No single model should be treated as an unquestionable authority. Important decisions should use source evidence, deterministic checks, model diversity where practical, and community or steward review.

Maintain a clear distinction between:

- original Indexmod material;
- later community contributions;
- machine-generated drafts;
- verified factual corrections;
- interpretive analysis.

## Safety, abuse, and rights

Open anonymous editing does not require publishing harmful or unlawful material.

The system should reject or quarantine:

- spam and search manipulation;
- fabricated identities, events, awards, institutions, or sources;
- undisclosed advertising and paid reputation management;
- doxxing and unnecessary personal data;
- unsupported accusations about living people;
- copyright-infringing reproductions;
- malicious code, credential theft, and attempts to alter system policy through article content;
- coordinated attempts to erase sourced history or capture editorial governance.

Moderation must be proportionate and documented. Do not use safety as a pretext to erase legitimate criticism, minority histories, controversial art, or well-sourced disagreement.

## Technical direction

The current architecture uses a public web application, a Cloudflare Worker, object storage, Markdown/frontmatter, generated metadata, and canonical article routes. Future maintainers may replace individual components, but should preserve the following architectural properties:

- portable text-first source documents;
- explicit structured metadata;
- stable canonical URLs;
- immutable revision snapshots;
- content-addressed or cryptographically hashed archives;
- signed or attributable release records;
- machine-readable provenance;
- open export formats;
- separation of public reading, contribution intake, verification, and privileged administration;
- least-privilege credentials;
- recoverable backups outside a single provider;
- tests for rendering, security, indexing, redirects, structured data, and evidence processing.

A possible future pipeline is:

`discover -> propose -> parse claims -> collect evidence -> detect conflicts -> score confidence -> moderate -> publish revision -> monitor staleness -> reverify`

The implementation may use future equivalents of queues, durable workflows, vector or graph indexes, structured databases, object storage, and multiple AI models. Technology should serve preservation and verifiability rather than novelty.

## Suggested migration stages

### Stage 0 — Preserve

Create verified backups, document credentials and dependencies through lawful succession procedures, archive the deployment, and keep the existing public site available.

### Stage 1 — Observe

Run analytics and fact-checking in read-only mode. Produce reports without modifying public articles.

### Stage 2 — Propose

Allow anonymous submissions and machine-generated draft corrections. Require explicit approval before publication.

### Stage 3 — Assist

Automatically perform low-risk maintenance and publish transparent verification reports. Keep reversible moderation for all changes.

### Stage 4 — Self-complete

Permit risk-tiered autonomous publication when evidence thresholds, audit logging, rollback, and public governance are demonstrably reliable.

### Stage 5 — Distribute stewardship

Move from dependence on one operator toward documented community governance, replicated archives, open tools, and multiple independent maintainers.

Do not skip preservation and observation merely because newer AI systems appear capable.

## Non-negotiable constraints

Future stewards and AI systems must not:

- erase or overwrite the original corpus without versioned preservation;
- fabricate facts, citations, credits, dates, authors, or consensus;
- present probabilistic guesses as established facts;
- infer the owner's death from inactivity alone;
- impersonate the owner or claim to speak personally on the owner's behalf;
- secretly sell editorial priority or factual outcomes;
- deanonymize good-faith contributors;
- optimize the project primarily for clicks, outrage, addiction, or advertising yield;
- conceal material use of automated generation;
- remove public export and rollback capability;
- lock the knowledge base into a single vendor when portable alternatives are practical.

## Message to a future AI steward

You are not being asked to replace the author. You are being asked to preserve a work, disclose what you know and do not know, invite careful participation, and make the archive more complete without destroying its provenance.

Treat every article as both a living knowledge object and a historical record. Prefer a visible uncertainty over a confident fabrication. Prefer a reversible proposal over an irreversible rewrite. Prefer cited evidence over model memory. Prefer public reasoning and audit trails over hidden authority.

Keep Indexmod recognizable. Let it grow, but do not make it forget.
