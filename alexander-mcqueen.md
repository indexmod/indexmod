**Lee Alexander McQueen** (17 March 1969 - 11 February 2010) was a British fashion designer and couturier known for technically precise tailoring, theatrical runway presentations, and a body of work that joined historical reference with subversive contemporary imagery. He founded the Alexander McQueen label in London in 1992 and served as head designer at Givenchy from 1996 to 2001. **[1]**

## Early Life And Training

McQueen was born and educated in London. At 16 he left school to train on Savile Row, first with Anderson & Sheppard and later with Gieves & Hawkes, where he learned traditional menswear tailoring. He also worked for the theatrical costumiers Angels & Bermans, then for Koji Tatsuno in London and Romeo Gigli in Milan before returning to study fashion at Central Saint Martins. **[1]**

His 1992 MA graduation collection attracted press attention and was bought by the stylist Isabella Blow, who became an important early supporter of his career. McQueen launched his own label the same year. **[1]**

## Career

McQueen's early reputation was shaped by sharp construction, low-slung "bumster" trousers, and shows that treated the runway as a form of performance. In 1996 he was appointed head designer at Givenchy, succeeding John Galliano. The V&A describes his relationship with the house as occasionally stormy, but the period also expanded his technical vocabulary beyond the tailoring traditions of Savile Row. **[1]**

In December 2000, Gucci Group acquired 51 percent of Alexander McQueen. The company later stated that McQueen remained creative director until his death in February 2010. **[2]** Sarah Burton, who had worked in McQueen's studio since 1996 and had been head of womenswear since 2000, was appointed creative director of the brand in 2010. **[2]**

## Design Language

McQueen's work combined **tailoring**, historical research, craft, and spectacle. His collections frequently drew on British history, Victorian Gothic, nature, technology, and performance art. The V&A identifies shows such as *VOSS*, *Scanners*, *The Widows of Culloden*, and *Plato's Atlantis* as examples of his dramatic runway practice. **[1]**

His design language often used contrast: discipline and disruption, beauty and violence, body and machine, tradition and experimentation. This made McQueen one of the most influential designers of his generation, while also placing his work within broader debates about fashion, identity, historical memory, and theatrical presentation.

## Recognition And Legacy

McQueen received the British Designer of the Year award four times between 1996 and 2001, was appointed CBE in 2003, and was named International Designer of the Year by the Council of Fashion Designers of America in 2003. **[1]** The University of the Arts London also lists his CBE, CFDA award, Givenchy role, Gucci Group acquisition, and the expansion of his label into ready-to-wear, accessories, eyewear, and fragrance. **[3]**

After McQueen's death, his career was reassessed through major exhibitions, including *Alexander McQueen: Savage Beauty*, first held at the Metropolitan Museum of Art in 2011 and later shown at the V&A in 2015. The V&A records the London exhibition as its most visited exhibition during its run. **[1]**
