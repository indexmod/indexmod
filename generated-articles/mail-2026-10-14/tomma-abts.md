***Updated 2026-10-14***

Tomma Abts is a German-born abstract painter based in London, known for compositions that investigate the relationship between geometric form, colour and pictorial space. Born in Kiel in 1967, she has lived in London since 1995 and received the Turner Prize in 2006. [1]

{{page:image}}

{{page:credit}}

## Painting

Abts develops her compositions gradually, allowing forms and colour relationships to emerge through successive stages of work. Her paintings can suggest spatial depth while retaining an emphatic sense of their constructed surface. [1]

The Hammer Museum's 2008 exhibition documents her use of acrylic and oil on compact canvases measuring 48 by 38 centimetres. Works included *Fewe* (2005), *Meko* (2006), *Welf* (2001) and *Lübbe* (2005). The presentation was accompanied by a catalogue with essays by Jan Verwoert, Bruce Hainley and Laura Hoptman. Her institutional exhibition history also includes Kunsthalle Basel, the Douglas Hyde Gallery in Dublin and the Van Abbemuseum in Eindhoven. [2]

## Fridericianum intervention

For the Fridericianum in Kassel in 2026, Abts extended her practice into a site-specific interior wall painting. The work responds to the second-floor rotunda through a circle, square, ellipse and rhombus. Their placement relates to the glass roof and rear wall, producing shifting impressions of foreground, background and shadow. [1]

The composition's colours, outlines and positions were developed on site before the institution's technical staff transferred the forms to the walls. The project makes the architecture part of the painting and is scheduled to remain on view until 3 January 2027. [1]
