Keith Piper is a British visual artist, researcher and academic whose work examines race, history and social relationships through painting, installation, moving image and digital media. Born in Malta in 1960 and raised in Birmingham, he was a founding member of the BLK Art Group and is associated with the development of Black British art. [1][2]

{{page:credit}}

## Background and practice

Piper studied at Trent Polytechnic and the Royal College of Art. His early interests in collage and print developed into experiments with computer technology, video editing and interactive media. The 1997 project *Relocating the Remains*, made for Iniva, included a CD-ROM and website, demonstrating how digital formats could become part of an installation-based practice. [1]

The BLK Art Group brought together artists including Piper, Eddie Chambers, Marlene Smith and Donald Rodney. Its exhibitions addressed Black British identity and the political conditions in which artists worked. Piper subsequently exhibited at institutions including Ikon in Birmingham, Camden Arts Centre in London and the New Museum in New York. [1][2]

His later practice also returns to earlier projects. *A Ship Called Jesus (Redux)*, a single-screen video made with Ikon in 2021, revisited themes from the original 1991 exhibition. [2]

## 22 Yards of Earth

For the 25th Biennale of Sydney in 2026, Piper presented *22 Yards of Earth*, a thirteen-minute looped video commissioned jointly by the Biennale and Samstag Museum of Art. An older West Indian man responds to an offscreen interrogator amid cricket memorabilia. The narrative uses Test cricket involving Australia, the West Indies and England from 1960 onwards to explore imperial history, national identification and class. [3]

The work was shown at Lewers: Penrith Regional Gallery during the Biennale. A subsequent Samstag presentation in Adelaide was scheduled for 9 October–4 December 2026. [3][4]
