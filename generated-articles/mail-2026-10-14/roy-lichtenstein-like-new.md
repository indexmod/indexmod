*Roy Lichtenstein: Like New* is an exhibition at the Whitney Museum of American Art in New York, scheduled from 11 October 2026 to 10 May 2027. The first major New York retrospective of Lichtenstein since 1993, it examines his treatment of images as material that can be copied, translated and transformed. [1][2]

{{page:image}}

{{page:credit}}

## Scope and curators

The exhibition brings together approximately 130 works spanning the early 1940s to the 1990s. Painting, drawing, collage, prints, sculpture and film are presented alongside recreated murals. It is organised by Whitney curator Meg Onli and guest curator Alex Da Corte, with Nakai Falcón and David Crane. [1]

Rather than limiting Lichtenstein to his best-known comic-derived paintings, the exhibition follows an extended investigation of perception, duplication and the circulation of images. Selected works include *Look Mickey* (1961), *Girl with Ball* (1961), *Drowning Girl* (1963) and *Artist's Studio “The Dance”* (1974). [1]

## Historical context

Lichtenstein was a central figure in Pop art, developing a distinctive visual language from advertisements, cartoons and commercial printing. His apparently mechanical surfaces involved extensive manual preparation, including drawings, projection, tracing and stencilled dots. In the mid-1960s he also treated the expressive brushstroke as an image that could be reconstructed in a deliberately impersonal style. [3]

*Like New* reunites the works from his 1962 exhibition at Leo Castelli Gallery and includes artists such as Louise Lawler, Richard Pettibone and Sturtevant to consider the wider history of copying and influence. [1][2]

## Fashion collaboration

The exhibition coincided with an eighteen-piece collaboration between Gap, the Whitney and the Estate of Roy Lichtenstein. Launched on 7 October 2026, it adapted the artist's imagery for clothing and accessories. The collection forms a separate retail project associated with the museum presentation. [4]
