***Updated 2026-10-14***

**Fridericianum** is a contemporary-art exhibition institution at Friedrichsplatz in Kassel, Germany. Founded in 1779 as a public museum, it is also a principal venue of documenta, the international exhibition established in Kassel in 1955. Its regular contemporary-art programme has operated since 1988.[1][2]

{{page:credit}}

## History and documenta

The eighteenth-century museum originally accommodated the collections of the Hessian landgrave and the princely state library. Its later association with documenta transformed it into a recurring setting for international contemporary-art exhibitions.[3]

The first documenta opened in July 1955 in the provisionally restored building. Founded by Arnold Bode, the exhibition subsequently developed into a major international series; the Fridericianum has remained a central venue. Between documenta editions, the building houses the institution’s own exhibition programme.[3][4]

## Contemporary programme

The Fridericianum presents contemporary art across more than 2,000 square metres. Its exhibitions encompass emerging practices and established artists, while its education programme is adapted to different audiences and age groups. Talks, performances and film screenings also form part of its activities, including the series *FF – Live at the Fridericianum*.[1]

Moritz Wesseler has directed the institution since November 2018. His programme includes artists receiving their first institutional presentations in Germany and the reconsideration of historical bodies of work. Previous directors include Veit Loers, René Block, Rein Wolfs and Susanne Pfeffer.[1]

## Selected programme in 2026

The institution’s 2026 programme included a Peter Fischli exhibition scheduled for 23 May–13 September. **Mohammed Sami: Hostless** is scheduled for 3 October 2026–3 January 2027, with an opening on 2 October. The latter exhibition focuses on interiors as spaces of memory and is being developed with S.M.A.K. in Ghent and Moderna Museet Malmö.[5][6]

## Education in 2026

In October 2026 the institution announced *Kunstzwerge*, also presented in English as *Art dwarfs*, for children aged approximately two and a half to five. The opening workshop, scheduled for 18 October, combines exploring the galleries with decorating fabric bags. German- and Russian-language sessions were announced. The series connects current exhibitions with creative play and activities for sensory perception and fine motor skills.[8]

Tomma Abts's architectural intervention, scheduled from 22 August 2026 to 3 January 2027, extends the exhibition programme into the second-floor rotunda.[9]
