***Updated 2026-10-14***

The Fridericianum is a contemporary art exhibition institution on Friedrichsplatz in Kassel, Germany. Founded in 1779 as a public museum, it has operated as a Kunsthalle since 1988, presenting temporary exhibitions across more than 2,000 square metres. Its programme brings together emerging practices, established artists and historical work reconsidered in a contemporary context. [1]

## Exhibition programme

Moritz Wesseler became director in November 2018, following directors including René Block, Rein Wolfs and Susanne Pfeffer. The institution describes his programme as particularly concerned with artists who have not previously received an institutional platform in Germany and with work deserving renewed attention. [1]

The programme extends beyond conventional gallery displays through interventions responding to the building. Tomma Abts's 2026 project, for example, occupies the second-floor rotunda: painted geometric forms establish relationships with its curved architecture, open space and glass roof. The intervention is scheduled from 22 August 2026 to 3 January 2027. [2]

## Education

Art education accompanies the exhibitions through guided visits and practical workshops adapted to different ages and interests. The institution's approach connects discussion of exhibited work with activities through which visitors can investigate artistic processes themselves. [3]

In October 2026 it announced *Kunstzwerge*, also presented in English as *Art dwarfs*, for children aged approximately two and a half to five. The opening workshop, scheduled for 18 October, combines exploration of the galleries with decorating fabric bags. German- and Russian-language sessions were announced. This is an educational programme within the Kunsthalle rather than a separate institution. [4]
